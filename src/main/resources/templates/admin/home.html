GETTING STARTED

What is the GATE examination?

The Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) is a national-level examination that primarily evaluates candidates' understanding of undergraduate-level subjects in engineering, technology, science, architecture, humanities and related disciplines.

GATE scores are used by participating institutions for admission to eligible postgraduate programmes. Some organisations also use GATE scores as part of their recruitment processes, subject to their own requirements.

The examination is conducted jointly by the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) and the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), on behalf of the National Coordination Board (NCB)-GATE, Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education, Government of India.

BEFORE YOU APPLY

Who can appear for GATE?

GATE eligibility depends on the examination's official rules for the relevant year. Generally, eligible candidates include students in the third year or higher of an approved undergraduate degree programme, as well as graduates in eligible disciplines.

  • Undergraduate students: Students meeting the minimum study-year requirement specified in the official notification.
  • Graduates: Candidates holding an eligible degree recognised under the applicable GATE rules.
  • Age limit: GATE has generally not imposed an upper age limit; verify the applicable year's official information.
  • Important: Degree eligibility, accepted qualifications and paper-selection rules should be checked in the official brochure before applying.

KNOW THE FORMAT

GATE exam pattern

The following are general features of the examination. Always check the relevant year's official brochure.

3h

Examination Duration

The standard GATE examination duration is three hours.

100

Total Marks

A standard GATE paper carries 100 marks.

MCQ

Question Types

Questions may include Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ), Multiple Select Questions (MSQ) and Numerical Answer Type (NAT) questions.

GA

General Aptitude

General Aptitude is an important component of the examination. Its marks distribution depends on the applicable paper and official pattern.

Marking scheme

MCQ questions may carry negative marks for incorrect answers. MSQ and NAT questions generally do not have negative marking. Exact question distribution and marking rules should be confirmed using the official notification for the relevant year.
